Florida Snowbird Departure Checklist: What to Do Before You Leave

A practical close-up list for seasonal Florida homeowners — water, HVAC, mail, storm prep, and insurance — then a path to scheduled Pinellas Home Watch if you do not want to rely on a neighbor.

This page is for the homeowner — the week you leave Florida, not the visit Knight Group makes after you are gone. Search demand for a Florida snowbird departure checklist or what to do before leaving a Florida house for the summer usually starts months before anyone is ready to hire a watcher. Use it as a close-up list, then decide whether you want scheduled photo reports while you are away.

It is not a licensed home inspection, not legal or insurance advice, and not a substitute for your carrier’s vacancy rules. If you want someone other than a neighbor to walk the house on a set schedule in Pinellas County, that is Knight Group Home Watch.

Water

  • Decide, in writing, whether the main stays on, is isolated, or is shut off — then tell whoever checks the house
  • Know where the main shutoff and hose bibs are
  • Fix drips you already know about; a slow drip becomes a ceiling stain while you are north
  • Note icemaker, washer, and outdoor hose connections

Thermostat and humidity

  • Set cooling for an unoccupied Gulf-coast house, not a lived-in setpoint
  • Confirm the system actually ran after you changed it
  • Replace a dirty filter before a long vacancy
  • Write the agreed temperature into your Home Watch protocol if you hire checks

Kitchen, refrigerator, and pests

  • Empty perishables; decide fridge on/off and freezer contents
  • Take out trash and wipe crumbs that invite pests
  • Unplug small appliances you do not want drawing power

Mail, packages, and the look of vacancy

  • Hold or forward mail; stop routine deliveries that pile up
  • Ask a neighbor or Home Watch to note flyers and packages
  • Do not advertise travel dates on social posts tied to the address

Storm and hurricane season

  • Bring in or secure outdoor furniture and loose items
  • Check that screens, doors, and obvious weak points are latched
  • Know your HOA’s storm rules if you live in a gated or deed-restricted community
  • Plan a post-storm check separately from weekly Home Watch

Windows, doors, vehicles, and access

  • Lock windows and doors; test the ones you actually use
  • Leave vehicles as your insurer and HOA allow — covered, moved, or listed
  • Write access instructions for a trusted person: gate, alarm, lockbox, HOA, and who to call first
  • Do not put alarm codes on a public web form

People to notify

  • Emergency contact who can authorize a repair
  • HOA or property manager if required
  • A licensed plumber or electrician you already trust, for work outside handyman scope

Insurance

  • Read any vacancy, unoccupied, secondary, or seasonal occupancy clause in your policy
  • Ask the carrier what visit frequency or documentation they want
  • Keep dated photos. They support a paper trail; they do not automatically pay a claim

FAQ

Departure questions

What you handle before you fly north, and how Pinellas Home Watch fits after you leave.

Is this the same as a Home Watch visit?

No. This page is for you, the homeowner, before you leave Florida. The Home Watch visit checklist is what Knight Group uses after you are gone. Use both: close the house well, then have scheduled photo reports while you are away.

Does completing this checklist replace Home Watch?

No. A neighbor or a one-time close-up cannot see a slow leak three weeks later. Home Watch is the scheduled follow-through in Pinellas County.

Will this satisfy my insurance vacancy clause?

Not by itself. Insurance policies differ in how they define vacant, unoccupied, secondary and seasonal residences. Review your policy forms or confirm requirements with your carrier or agent before leaving. Knight Group reports support a paper trail; they do not determine coverage or guarantee a claim outcome.

Should I shut the water off?

Follow your plumber’s and insurer’s advice for that house. Many snowbirds shut the main or isolate unused branches and leave HVAC running for humidity. Write the water setting into your Home Watch protocol so visits match what you intended.

What if I am leaving a Lutz or North Tampa house?

Handyman work can be quoted there on expanding routes with address confirmation. Home Watch stays Pinellas-first for now because recurring visits need route density. Pinellas second homes still use the Home Watch Pinellas page.
